Step 2: The Diagnostic ScriptQuestion 1: The Baseline"Do you struggle with confidence on the basketball court?"[A] Yes (Routes to Question 2)[B] No (Routes to Advanced Pressure & Game Conversion)Question 2: The Foundation (Earned vs. Unearned)"Be honest with yourself: have you actually put in the serious hours and deliberate reps to expect the ball to go in every time?"   [Option A — No]: "Truthfully, no. I play when I can, but I haven't put in consistent, disciplined shooting reps every week."   $\rightarrow$ Diagnosis: You Haven't Earned the Right to Be Confident Yet.   Core Principle: Confidence is not a mindset hack or a motivational quote; confidence is a byproduct of proof. If you haven't put in thousands of reps, your subconscious knows it, and it will panic under live game speed. You don't have a mental block; you have a volume deficit.   Prescribed Workouts:The 100-3's Structured Make Goal: 5 spots around the perimeter, 20 makes each, tracking clean makes instead of attempts.   40 Mid-Range Pull-Up Ladder: 20 going right, 20 going left, focusing strictly on stopping on balance.   Handlz 15-Minute Daily Ball-Handling Workout: Heavy finger-pad pound series to build automatic ball control.[Option B — Yes]: "Yes. I’ve put in the reps, spent hundreds of hours in the gym, and knock down shots in empty gyms, but I still get in my head and lose it when it matters."   $\rightarrow$ Diagnosis: The True Confidence Bottleneck (Routes to Question 3)   Question 3: Pinpointing the Mental Leak"Where does your confidence break down the most?"[Choice 1 — Pre-Game Jitters & Physical Tension]: "Before games or big runs, my stomach drops, my hands feel cold, my heart races, and my muscles feel stiff before I even take a shot."   $\rightarrow$ Outcome 1: Pre-Game Nerves & Arousal Control[Choice 2 — The External Weight / Social Fear]: "I’m terrified of making mistakes in front of the coach, getting benched, getting judged by teammates/family, or getting thrown off by refs and trash talk."   $\rightarrow$ Outcome 2: External vs. Internal Overload[Choice 3 — Result-Oriented Ghost Slump]: "If I miss two shots in a row, my rhythm falls apart. I start obsessing over percentages, and my jumper feels completely broken."   $\rightarrow$ Outcome 3: Outcome-Focused FragilityStep 3: The 3 Targeted Solutions (Philosophy + Drill)Outcome 1: Pre-Game Jitters & Physical TensionThe Core Lesson: The cold water doesn't get warmer the longer you wait. You don't eliminate nerves by sitting in the locker room hoping they go away; you lean directly into the physical discomfort. "Hold the fear in one hand, take the step forward with the other."   The 4-Step Pre-Game Protocol:Audio Anchor: Put your headphones on and play the music that locks you into your zone (blaring or calming).   Active Sweat: You must be sweating before you take your first official shot. Cold nerves lead to tight jumpers.   Physical Reset: Drop and hit 10 crisp pushups to pull nervous energy into your chest and shoulders.Box Breathing: Take 4 deep belly breaths (4 seconds in, 4 seconds hold, 4 seconds out, 4 seconds hold) to calm the vagus nerve.   The Actionable Drill:Sprints into 2 Pressure Free Throws: Full-court baseline sprint, run straight to the free-throw line with your chest heaving, control your breathing, and knock down 2 consecutive free throws. Miss one, repeat the sprint.   Outcome 2: External Factors (Coaches, Fans, Refs, Teammates)The Core Lesson (From the Waterfall Doc): You cannot control when the coach puts you in, how long you play, what the defense does, or what the ref calls. When you step on the court worried about external factors, you are comparing apples to oranges. You have to "put the blinders on" and retreat into your own internal world. When you play with close friends, you play loose and fearless because you don't care about looking bad. Teleport into that exact frame of mind no matter who is sitting in the stands.   The 1 Master Cue: "Put the blinders on—control the breath, trust the reps, stay in your world."   The Actionable Drill:The "Blinders-On" 1v1 or Pickup Test Rep: Enter pickup runs or 1v1 situations where your only goal is to hunt discomfort. You are not allowed to pass out of open shots or hide in the corner to avoid mistakes. When the ball touches your hands, you shoot with zero hesitation and let the result go.   Outcome 3: Outcome-Focused Fragility (The Ghost Slump)The Core Lesson (From the Waterfall Doc): "I’d rather be 0 for 10 feeling like I'm shooting the ball great with a chance at a game winner, than 9 for 10 feeling like my jump shot is garbage." Amateurs judge a shot purely by whether the net moved. Elite shooters judge a shot strictly by rhythm, release, and physical feel. If the release felt clean and pure, an in-and-out miss is irrelevant—trust the practice and shoot the next one with identical form.   The 1 Master Cue: "Execution over outcome—judge the feeling, not the rim."   The Actionable Drills:Feel-Based Warmup (Swish Feedback): Start from 5 feet; count only swishes, completely ignoring banked makes or ugly rim rolls. Swishes are immediate physical proof that your kinetic line was pure.   Mid-Range 2-Dribble Pull-Up (Feel Scoring): Shoot 10 mid-range pull-ups going left and 10 going right. Do not count makes or misses; score each shot from 1 to 5 based purely on how smooth the momentum and release felt.   Follow-Through Threes (Freeze the Frame): Shoot 20 threes; freeze your wrist in the follow-through for 3 full seconds after release regardless of whether the shot went in or missed.
