The intended new player onboarding starts with the familiar sign-in screen. Players choosing to create an account enter email and password first, then complete a separate profile step with their name, training frequency as a count per week or month, usual session length, and next planned session date/time. The preview uses a calendar popover for scheduling. Completing that step takes them into the Hub.

The seven-day journey is embedded in a personalized Hub; schedule each step from the player's training frequency and next-session date rather than assuming consecutive calendar days. Keep the seven dated progress bars full-width and visually blended into the background, without labeling the experience as onboarding; the active bar should have a bright PLAYR1 Pro-orange glow. Place the training rhythm at the upper right with a circular editor for frequency and session length, and show the next session as a prominent orange pill that opens a calendar and time picker. Keep the calendar control beside the day strip. Day 1 opens with “Welcome to The PLAYR1 Project,” then a borderless YouTube-like trailer preview whose channel name is “The PLAYR1 Project” and whose avatar, title, and views/date metadata are editable. Follow it with a responsive holographic shooting chart titled “Here's where your shooting could be”: make the graph fill the card background, use green/yellow/red lines, keep copy minimal, and separate projected percentages so they do not bunch at line endpoints. Illustrative endpoints are 80% threes, 70% mid-range, and 90% free throws, with varied monthly points rather than a smooth climb. Put a translucent glass “Log Your Baseline” button in the chart's upper right; opening it shows the three session sizes and color-matched shot-count breakdowns, and starting a choice opens the baseline workout logger. Use direct numeric fields for entering makes, colored to match each chart line rather than +/- controls. Keep the three baseline volumes with Balanced (115 attempts) selected by default, let the selected volume affect the projection curve. Completing the baseline reveals its real starting points and teases the next diagnostic. Baseline volumes are 100 threes + 40 mid-range pull-ups + 25 free throws (165 attempts); 50 + 40 + 25 (115); or 50 + 20 + 15 (85). The colorful one-year chart is illustrative and must not imply guaranteed results.
Day 2 keeps the same scheduled-day header, marks Day 1 complete, highlights Day 2, and shows the second training date calculated from the player's cadence. Its only two main experiences are a Coach P introduction and the Workout #2 preview, with no Day 2 video. The compact Coach P introduction should use the “Talk to Coach P” prompt, a person-like coach avatar, and animated voice-wave lines inspired by the Coach P prompt screen. The workout is “Find Your Shooting Range,” created from six real system drills in the shared drill library and linked as a system workout: Find Your Range (30 shots), Elbow Jumpers (20), Mid-Range Pull-ups (40), Catch & Shoot 3s (25), Free Throws (10), plus bonus Finishing & Ball Handling (40 layups and 5 cone sets). Preserve the supplied drill instructions: advance a step after two makes, step forward after two misses; 10 shots at each elbow; 8 shots at each of five mid-range spots split 4 left/4 right; 5 catch-and-shoot or solo stationary shots at each of five three-point spots; 10 game-routine free throws; bonus 10 regular layups, 10 inside-hand layups on each side, and zig-zag cone dribbling using crossover, between-the-legs, or behind-the-back moves. Tapping the preview opens the complete graphic/details and the existing logger. In both the real and onboarding Hub headers, place the player's profile picture to the left of the date and greeting, with a subtle white glowing ring.

**Why:** the user wants the Hub journey to reveal useful actions in stages, while keeping the familiar Hub shell and a consistent player identity.

**How to apply:** reuse stored player-setup answers and existing Hub workout routes rather than inventing new question choices or a separate workout system. In the onboarding preview, let the admin switch between the built Day 1 and Day 2 views without leaving preview mode.

The current admin preview should focus on the first-session experience inside the existing Hub shell; do not add placeholder days unless requested again. Keep the preview immersive by hiding app navigation and using a compact page selector to move between sign-in, account creation, profile, and the built Day 1 and Day 2 Hub views, with a nearby X to exit back to Admin. Keep the Hub visually consistent with PLAYR1's black-and-orange app experience, with clear hierarchy and interactive baseline feedback. Keep the new onboarding sequence separate from the current live onboarding until the user explicitly asks to migrate it; Day 2's requested drills/workout are real shared library content and should launch through the existing logger. The public lead-magnet quiz remains a separate experience.

**Why:** the user wants an immersive path that reuses the familiar account-entry aesthetic, asks schedule questions after account credentials, and then keeps the first-session experience inside the Hub with an motivating progress visualization and useful first-day interactions.

**How to apply:** preserve the exact baseline counts above; do not change live onboarding while iterating on the preview. At the start of Day 2, show the player-profile questions as a full-screen step styled like the sign-up/profile pages, then continue into the Day 2 Hub; do not place them in a Day 2 Hub card. Keep Years Played as a numeric field and allow repeated choices across the four skill-ranking questions. Keep the Day 1 Hub preview within one phone viewport without page scrolling, including when training frequency or session length changes; keep cadence details on one compact line and distribute the chart, summary, and shooting cards through the available height. Use the white graphic/details treatment as the pattern for future workout previews. Reuse the existing AuthPage and actual Hub shell, keep account creation and profile questions visually consistent with the sign-in screen, use styled dropdowns and centered dialogs for schedule edits, list all seven sessions vertically, and show a month grid with a separate time control after a session is selected. Let players edit the next session plus each planned date and time from the Hub calendar. Keep the three shooting summaries compact and polished. Show the real five-tab player navigation and a feedback action in the Hub preview, while overlaying the preview page selector so it does not push the Hub greeting down. Keep every preview page under the onboarding-preview route, and use the page selector plus its adjacent exit control as the only preview controls. Preview submissions stay local and never call auth APIs.

## Current live flow and lead-magnet boundary

The public lead-magnet quiz happens outside the PLAYR1 app and remains independent. The current in-app setup is still a separate five-step flow, and new accounts are created only at step 5 after player details are collected. Incomplete setup still gates player access after login.

**Why:** the day-based experience is being designed in the admin preview first; the existing live flow remains in place until the user asks for rollout.

**How to apply:** keep quiz data and app-setup completion separate; only use matching quiz answers as optional prefill, never treat quiz completion as app-setup completion.

## Retired setup answers

When a setup field is retired, remove it from the current prompts, validation, and new completion payloads, but preserve its previously saved value when completing setup.

**Why:** the user asked to stop collecting customized-drill email without deleting older saved setup data.

**How to apply:** keep legacy answer keys in storage merges, but do not repopulate them from account data or write new values; keep the public lead-magnet quiz separate.
